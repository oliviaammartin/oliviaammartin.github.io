## Research and Outreach Interests

I am a Ph.D. candidate in the Department of Chemistry and Chemical Biology at Rutgers University, working under the supervision of Dr. Richard Remsing in the Remsing Lab. My research uses **molecular dynamics simulations and statistical mechanics theory to investigate how the structure and dynamics of water and small molecules, like simple lipids, are perturbed by mineral surfaces**. Specifically, my dissertation examines water's unique hydrogen-bonding structure and dynamics at mineral surfaces arising from nuclear quantum effects. Building on this understanding of the water-mineral interface, I predict simple lipid micelle and bilayer formation at mineral surfaces using advanced computational techniques and statistical mechanics theories. **This work has led to predicting the hydrophilic, hydrophilic, hydrophobic, and entropic driving forces for lipid assembly at mineral surfaces, including how H-bonding and hydrophobic driving forces differ between different crystal cuts of α-alumina.**

Beyond my dissertation research, I am committed to **research mentoring and science communication within the Rutgers community and beyond**. During my Ph.D., I have organized multiple conferences and seminars, including serving as the <a href="https://www.grc.org/chemistry-and-physics-of-liquids-grs-conference/2027/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Chemistry and Physics of Liquids GRS</a> Co-Chair and as an Organizer and Director of Fundraising and Communications for the 2026 <a href="https://www.abgradcon.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Astrobiology Graduate Conference</a>. 

I am currently **seeking a postdoctoral position beginning in the Summer/Fall of 2027**, where I hope to apply and extend my skills in molecular dynamics simulations, statistical mechanics, and free energy calculations to complex interfacial systems.
