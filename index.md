**Currently seeking a position as a Postdoctoral Researcher starting Summer/Fall 2027.**

## Research and Outreach Interests

I am a Ph.D. candidate in the Department of Chemistry and Chemical Biology at Rutgers University, working under the supervision of Dr. Richard Remsing in the Remsing Lab. My research uses **molecular dynamics simulations and statistical mechanics theory to investigate how the structure and dynamics of water and small molecules, like simple lipids, are perturbed by mineral surfaces**. Specifically, my dissertation examines water's unique hydrogen-bonding structure and dynamics at mineral surfaces arising from nuclear quantum effects. Building on this understanding of the water-mineral interface, I predict simple lipid micelle and bilayer formation at mineral surfaces using advanced computational techniques and statistical mechanics theories. This work has led to a **new framework for computing the free energies of micelle and bilayer formation in water and at water-mineral interfaces**.

Beyond my dissertation research, I am committed to **research mentoring and science communication within the Rutgers community and beyond**. During my Ph.D., I have organized multiple conferences and seminars, including serving as the Chemistry representative of the Rutgers Chemical Physics Early-Career Campus Committee and as Director of Fundraising and Communications for the 2026 Astrobiology Graduate Conference.

I am currently **seeking postdoctoral positions beginning in Summer/Fall 2027**, where I hope to apply and extend my skills in molecular dynamics simulations, statistical mechanics, and free energy calculations to water-protein and protein-protein interfaces. With this foundation, I aim to **investigate the microscopic characteristics at these interfaces and how they shape the macroscopic behavior of protein-protein and protein-drug interactions**.
